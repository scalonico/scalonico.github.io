# Causal Evaluation of AI-Assisted Decisions

## Project objective

This project develops econometric methods to evaluate AI systems that influence high-stakes decisions. The central question is:

> When an AI system changes a recommendation or eligibility decision, what is its causal effect on human behavior, outcomes, inequality, and social welfare?

The project studies AI as part of a decision system—not as an isolated prediction model. Its initial empirical domain is health care, with later applications to public administration and social policy.

## Proposed contribution

The first paper will connect three objects that are usually studied separately:

1. An estimated AI score and operational decision threshold.
2. Human discretion between the recommendation and final action.
3. The marginal welfare value of expanding or contracting algorithmic eligibility.

The baseline design uses a discontinuity in recommendations at an algorithmic score threshold. The methodological contribution must go beyond a conventional fuzzy regression discontinuity design. Candidate extensions include learned running variables, changing model versions, capacity constraints, group-specific thresholds, and sequential post-deployment monitoring.

## Current empirical lead

UC Davis Health's BE-FAIR population-health model is the leading candidate. The published application has the required institutional structure:

- A predicted probability of future emergency-department use or hospitalization.
- An operational risk-percentile threshold motivated by staff capacity.
- Human review before outreach and care-management enrollment.
- Downstream utilization outcomes.
- Explicit equity and model-monitoring objectives.

Feasibility depends on whether patient-level score histories, flags, human actions, treatment intensity, model versions, and outcomes can be linked.

An availability audit completed on August 11, 2026 found no public patient-level release. UC Davis Health has formal internal routes for de-identified OMOP data, identified EHR data after IRB approval, and curated operational extracts. The unresolved bottleneck is whether historical BE-FAIR scores and workflow actions were retained for all scored patients, especially those below the operational cutoff. See the [data strategy](./data-options.md) for the full assessment and contact sequence.

## Paper sequence

### Paper 1: Inference at algorithmic decision boundaries

Develop identification and inference for the causal and marginal-policy effects of recommendations generated by learned scores.

### Paper 2: Do AI-generated care-management recommendations improve outcomes?

Estimate effects on human decisions, program participation, emergency-department use, hospitalization, costs, and disparities.

### Paper 3: Sequential causal audits for deployed AI systems

Develop inference for outcome effects across time, model versions, institutions, outcomes, and prespecified population groups.

## Current status

The baseline model is specified at version 0.2. The central methodological direction is two-source inference at learned policy boundaries. The immediate priorities are:

1. Extend the simulation to repeated training samples and compare conditional with training-aware confidence intervals.
2. Conduct a feasibility conversation with the UC Davis Health analytics and population-health teams using the outreach package.
3. Obtain a data dictionary or aggregate score/action counts before seeking patient-level data.
4. Search the closest econometric literature on generated running variables, sample splitting, and random policy boundaries.
